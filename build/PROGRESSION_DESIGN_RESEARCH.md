# Eco progression: unlimited stats, capped items and a hybrid

Research date: **2026-10-26 (Australia/Sydney)**. Audited local source: `732080a`, branch `codex/v0.4.4`; working tree clean at intake. This report is a **proposal, not implementation approval**. Ownership for this session is this report, material reconciliation findings, and retirement of the completed research prompt. No runtime, tuning, assets, profile, save, dependency or frozen knight pack change is selected.

> Historical source snapshot: this report audits `732080a`, not the current
> V0.4.5 build. The game now has four weapon slots, seven-level weapon paths,
> one Cleanup Vacuum and an active-wall clock. References below to two slots,
> Cyclone evolution and half-speed time describe that older baseline. Re-audit
> those assumptions before selecting any experiment; no proposed passive system
> is implemented or approved. Included in the delivery record on 2026-10-29.

## Recommendation in one minute

Prototype **three capped passive slots alongside the existing two automatic tool slots**, with deterministic authored levels during the normal run. Preserve the current readable Vacuum evolution: acquisition, four real reach/capacity picks, then same-slot evolution. If the player explicitly chooses Endless after the final boss, permit a restricted stat-stacking outlet once construction of the chosen build is complete. Keep the passive exclusions for that entire run.

This is design 3 below. Test its authored-run portion against today's system before deciding whether to ship anything. Use existing names and stable IDs, not a new equipment catalogue or art campaign. A Safety Gear label already exists; the consequential change is its finite authored progression **and the slot it occupies**.

The strongest counterargument is that short runs may already have enough opportunity cost through scarce picks. A three-slot commitment could turn an unlucky early offer into regret, while removing quality rolls could remove the most exciting moments. Clearer current cards and offer control might solve much of the problem more cheaply. The prototype must earn its complexity through comprehension, varied choices and replay motivation, not merely produce tidy inventories.

**A pick is not a skill level.** [Tier scaling](../src/game/systems/upgrades/upgrade-tiers.ts), line 140, uses `Math.round(multiplier)`: common/uncommon/rare grant 1 skill level, epic/legendary grant 2, and **Unique 2.5× grants 3**, subject to real skill cap. Some comments still describe only two. Thus Fragmentation, Cleanup Streak and Sweep can each cap in two Unique selections; the theoretical minimum finite selections to exhaust all finite entries is **25**, versus 35 at common. This is a combinatorial bound, not a likely run. Eligibility checks actual skill levels to stop offering an already-full pure skill before its pick cap. Described weapon-track badges use `picks + 1`, whereas the skill has its own level; Sweep's track badge can therefore differ from skill rank. A capped design must pick one explicit level authority rather than reuse that ambiguity.

Six skills remain defined, but only **three have acquisition offers in Eco**: Fragmentation, Cleanup Streak and owner-only Collection Sweep. Sorting Momentum, Compaction Burst and Cascade are parked, as are projectile pierce/count offers. Their presence in copy/definitions does not put them in the current selectable pool. Do not revive them as a convenient item roster.

Every level queues a paused, mandatory choice of **three distinct identities**. Selection prefers no more than two from a category when alternatives exist; there is no reserved weapon card, pity timer, reroll, banish, hold or skip. The scene resets a **fixed upgrade seed** on restart (lines 426/953), so equivalent choices/luck histories reproduce offers; random offers alone do not establish varied replay experiences.

### Where choices dilute

A fully entitled opening can mix 18 eligible identities across stats, skills, risk and tool work. Only three are shown, and nine stats keep occupying offers forever. At luck0 the opening contains ten common, seven rare and one epic identity, yielding weight **1445** (10×100 +7×60 +25). This count includes acquisition and two starter tracks; secondary reach/evolution are initially excluded. Category filtering makes exact three-card inclusion probabilities state-dependent; a simple `3/N` estimate is not an offer guarantee.

Damage and cycling affect both tools, while owned reach affects only one. A high-quality general card can sensibly displace a planned common track pick. Luck has delayed payoff, HP has immediate recovery, and risk choices compete for the same scarce level. Capped skills/risks eventually depart, concentrating the residual pool on stats. Quality changes both magnitude and the number of skill ranks delivered, so comparing cards requires role, units, owner, current bonus, quality and future prerequisites. Existing names convey identity, but not durable exclusions.

Current sources of identity are investment order, finite run budget, risk accepted and tool development. In sufficient Endless play the player can buy all finite offers and continue every stat axis. Caps without slots would merely make “take everything” end sooner.

## 3. Comparison of the three Eco designs

Design 1 is today's mixed pool, with unlimited stat axes and quality. Design 2 below adds the proposed finite passive slots and deterministic item levels; after construction is exhausted it offers recovery/finish rather than numerical escalation. Design 3 uses the same construction but adds the explicit Endless outlet in section 6. Merely adding caps without slots is a separate, weak variant: it improves completion feedback but retains eventual universal builds.

| Criterion | 1. Current unlimited stats + quality | 2. Finite named capped items + slots | 3. Capped construction + Endless outlet |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Accessibility / cognitive load | No irreversible item commitment, but units, additive bonuses, owner tracks and quality demand repeated comparisons | Three visible slots and `level/cap` give a small plan; names alone will not teach armour or additive percentages. More commitment anxiety | Same clear opening, plus one explained transition; adding the outlet early would defeat the simplification |
| Consequences / identity | Every pick costs time/power elsewhere, but no lasting passive exclusion | Choosing three of eleven excludes eight identities throughout the run. Caps alone do not supply this exclusion | Same exclusions; growth continues only through selected axes, so Endless cannot buy the missing eight |

## 4. Proposed passive roster and tool rules

**All caps, fixed-level semantics, slot rules and numbers in this section are proposed.** Names already in `copy.ts` are retained; no new passive identities are needed for the first prototype. Level one includes its effect and occupies one of **three passive slots**. Later levels occupy no extra slot. No selling, swapping, slot expansion, hidden reward-slot bypass or evolution slot refund. Passive acquisition requires an empty passive slot; with slots full, only owned nonmaxed passives remain eligible. Passives never occupy tool slots. Taking three survival/utility choices is allowed, rather than enforcing an offensive recipe.

These are candidate **cumulative bonuses at each item level**, relative to the preset-resolved starting state. They are not percentages of the current value. Default quality becomes fixed for this construction variant; do not secretly apply today's tier multiplier as well.

## 6. Selection, exhaustion and late growth contract

The following are proposed rules, not features shipped by this report.

**Authored construction:** keep three distinct offers, current appearance weights and category preference for the first comparison. Remove quality multipliers from items, skills and tool construction; fixed labels must not pretend a zero-effect quality roll adds value. Each selection increments exactly one authored item/skill level. Acquisition/evolution/world bargains stay fixed. This also reduces Sharp Eye's value, an explicit risk. Do not compensate by buffing luck or XP in the same experiment.

Eligibility order: entitlement → owner/prerequisite → passive slot/ownership → actual item or skill cap → optional banish → distinct draw. Validate stale clicks at application time, never spend a pending credit on a no-op. Slots full removes unowned passive candidates; levelling or evolution never reopens a slot. Keep random offers rather than a shopping catalogue; display three slot occupants, exact next increment and a plain commitment notice before acquisition. A higher-level passive must not increase its appearance weight merely by being owned without a separately tested rule.

**Pool exhaustion is mandatory prototype work.** Replace the current “need three or throw” assumption: display `min(3, eligible count)` real choices. If fewer than three remain, append one recovery action only when injured (proposed restore10% max HP, no permanent effect); also permit “Continue without gain.” With zero useful choices at full HP, consume each earned choice explicitly or through a clearly announced batch action. Never reopen excluded items, invent three duplicate cards, grant a risk automatically, silently discard pending levels or force a site bargain. Normal clearing can still collect XP and must obey the same policy. Design2 stops numerical growth here; it does not pretend a healing reward scales damage.

**Offer-control follow-up, separate from first A/B:** propose two run-local rerolls and one banish available without a new unlock. A reroll spends one charge and redraws identities; exclude the previous draw if enough legal alternatives exist, otherwise disclose possible repeats. No quality fishing in construction. A banish removes one **unowned passive identity** for this run, costs a charge but not a level-up, and redraws; cannot free an occupied slot or remove an evolution prerequisite. No per-run permanent catalogue filtering, hold or paid shop. Exhaustion fallback is never banishable. Compare this against both arms if added: otherwise an improved result would confound caps with better offer control. Current game has none of these controls.

**Hybrid outlet trigger:** `mode === endless`, reached through the existing explicit post-final-boss decision, **and** three passive slots occupied with every chosen passive maxed, every track on owned tools maxed (including Sweep), and any owned Vacuum evolved. A starter-only build need not acquire Vacuum to complete construction. Optional world bargains do not block the trigger or get auto-accepted. Before this predicate, Endless continues ordinary construction. No trigger at an arbitrary player level, elapsed minute or first maxed item. Never require a third unavailable slot/item if future banish/unlock changes make filling impossible; validation must guarantee enough eligible passive identities, or provide an explicit owner-approved build-seal rule in that later phase.

After trigger, freeze passive membership; growth candidates are selected passive axes only. Initial proposed fixed increments: Reinforced Tools +0.10 damage bonus, Safety Gear +10 max/current HP, Protective Kit +4 armour, Field Medic +0.1 regen. **Do not extend attack speed, crit, range, primary targets, Sweep frequency, luck or movement/pickup geometry in the first outlet experiment.** This protects readable cadence and a simple numeric scope. When a chosen trio has no eligible outlet axis (e.g. Boots/Collection Range/Precision Sort), offer a common weapon-damage growth action (+1 base damage to one owned tool, chosen by card) plus recovery/continue. This universal fallback does not acquire a missing passive, but does soften exclusions; test it separately before release. More generally, weapon-damage fallback is available only when no selected passive can grow, not as a route to optimal mixed-axis farming.

Offer up to three distinct eligible growth identities (or tool IDs for that fallback), using equal weights and no quality for the initial test. No auto-pick or random-always mode is included in the first prototype. A later opt-in “random eligible growth” can reduce repetitive late interruptions, must show its policy, and must never select equipment/risk/evolution/banish on the player's behalf. Continue bounded finite-number checks; “uncapped” means no authored level cap, not permission for Infinity/NaN. Performance caps, actor geometry and effects stay independent of growth values.
